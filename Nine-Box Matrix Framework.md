# Accessibility and Inclusion GE–McKinsey Nine-Box Matrix Framework for Digital Products and Services

## Purpose

This document defines a governance-oriented GE–McKinsey Nine-Box Matrix methodology for evaluating accessibility, disability inclusion, and inclusive design across digital products, services, business units, operational domains, and strategic portfolios.

The framework is designed to function as a reusable Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) knowledge document for AI systems responsible for:

- portfolio governance
- strategic investment analysis
- operating model evaluation
- transformation planning
- product portfolio management
- accessibility maturity analysis
- inclusion governance
- digital strategy
- innovation prioritisation
- enterprise risk evaluation

The methodology positions accessibility and inclusion as strategic portfolio evaluation dimensions rather than isolated compliance activities.

The framework assumes the organisation provides commercial digital services and seeks to maximise engagement, usability, retention, participation, and long-term market resilience across:

- disabled users
- neurodivergent users
- D/deaf users
- ageing populations
- users with temporary impairments
- users experiencing situational or environmental barriers

The framework evaluates how accessibility contributes to:

- market competitiveness
- customer retention
- operational resilience
- innovation capability
- governance maturity
- procurement strength
- customer trust
- long-term commercial sustainability

---

# Strategic Positioning of Accessibility Within the GE–McKinsey Matrix

Traditional GE–McKinsey analysis evaluates business units against two dimensions:

1. Industry Attractiveness
2. Business Strength

This framework extends the model by evaluating how accessibility and inclusion influence both dimensions simultaneously.

The methodology assumes inaccessible systems reduce:

- market competitiveness
- customer engagement
- procurement success
- demographic reach
- operational quality
- innovation maturity
- customer trust
- workforce sustainability

At the same time, inclusive systems strengthen:

- market resilience
- demographic adaptability
- usability consistency
- customer loyalty
- procurement competitiveness
- operational quality
- innovation capability
- strategic sustainability

Accessibility therefore becomes a strategic portfolio capability that influences investment prioritisation and business unit evaluation.

---

# Accessibility-Centred GE–McKinsey Matrix Structure

The framework evaluates products, services, platforms, departments, or operational domains across two dimensions:

| Dimension | Description |
|---|---|
| Industry / Market Attractiveness | The long-term strategic value of accessibility within a market or operational environment |
| Accessibility and Inclusion Business Strength | The organisation’s capability to deliver inclusive, accessible, resilient, and trusted experiences |

These dimensions combine to create nine portfolio positions used to guide:

- investment strategy
- remediation prioritisation
- transformation planning
- innovation focus
- governance intervention
- operational restructuring

Industry attractiveness evaluates how strategically important accessibility and inclusion are within a market, service area, operational domain, or customer ecosystem.

# Strategic Interpretation of the Nine Boxes

# 1. High Attractiveness / High Strength

## Strategic Position

### Invest and Accelerate

These business units or products demonstrate:

- strong accessibility maturity
- high customer relevance
- strong retention opportunity
- procurement competitiveness
- scalable inclusion capability

---

## Strategic Actions

- Increase investment
- Expand inclusive innovation
- Use as organisational exemplars
- Scale accessibility systems
- Introduce predictive inclusion analytics
- Expand market reach aggressively

---

# 2. High Attractiveness / Medium Strength

## Strategic Position

### Strengthen and Scale

These areas have strong market opportunity but inconsistent accessibility capability.

---

## Strategic Actions

- Accelerate governance maturity
- Improve accessibility operations
- Expand inclusive design systems
- Increase workforce capability
- Prioritise remediation investment

---

# 3. High Attractiveness / Low Strength

## Strategic Position

### Critical Transformation Required

These represent high-risk, high-opportunity environments.

Accessibility weaknesses create:

- major customer risk
- procurement exposure
- reputational vulnerability
- long-term commercial instability

---

## Strategic Actions

- Prioritise executive intervention
- Conduct accessibility transformation programmes
- Redesign governance structures
- Introduce inclusion analytics
- Modernise delivery systems
- Reduce critical customer friction urgently

---

# 4. Medium Attractiveness / High Strength

## Strategic Position

### Optimise and Expand

Accessibility capability is strong, though market pressure is less intense.

---

## Strategic Actions

- Improve operational efficiency
- Reuse accessibility capabilities elsewhere
- Expand inclusion innovation gradually
- Use as capability incubators

---

# 5. Medium Attractiveness / Medium Strength

## Strategic Position

### Prioritise Improvements

Moderate opportunity and moderate maturity require selective optimisation.

---

## Strategic Actions

- Improve consistency
- Reduce operational inefficiencies
- Increase accessibility governance integration
- Strengthen analytics maturity

---

# 6. Medium Attractiveness / Low Strength

## Strategic Position

### Selective Intervention

Accessibility weaknesses exist, but strategic urgency is lower.

---

## Strategic Actions

- Focus on high-risk journeys
- Improve governance selectively
- Address critical usability failures
- Rationalise investment carefully

---

# 7. Low Attractiveness / High Strength

## Strategic Position

### Maintain Selectively

Accessibility capability is strong, but market opportunity is limited.

---

## Strategic Actions

- Maintain baseline maturity
- Avoid overinvestment
- Reuse capability across portfolio areas
- Monitor future demographic shifts

---

# 8. Low Attractiveness / Medium Strength

## Strategic Position

### Rationalise Carefully

Moderate capability exists within low strategic priority environments.

---

## Strategic Actions

- Limit major investment
- Maintain essential compliance and usability
- Focus on operational efficiency

---

# 9. Low Attractiveness / Low Strength

## Strategic Position

### Minimise Investment Exposure

These areas demonstrate weak strategic value and weak accessibility capability.

---

## Strategic Actions

- Minimise unnecessary investment
- Reduce operational risk exposure
- Maintain minimum accessibility obligations
- Consider decommissioning or consolidation

# Accessibility Portfolio Evaluation Principles

# Accessibility Must Influence Strategic Investment

The framework assumes accessibility capability should directly influence:

- investment prioritisation
- transformation sequencing
- innovation focus
- procurement strategy
- product roadmap decisions

Accessibility maturity is therefore treated as a strategic portfolio asset.

---

# Exclusion Creates Hidden Portfolio Risk

The framework assumes inaccessible systems create hidden exposure including:

- customer attrition
- reputational instability
- operational inefficiency
- procurement failure
- workforce friction
- long-term demographic misalignment

These risks compound across portfolios over time.

---

# Accessibility Enables Long-Term Resilience

Business units with strong accessibility maturity are typically more:

- adaptable
- usable
- consistent
- trusted
- operationally resilient
- customer-centred

Accessibility therefore strengthens long-term strategic sustainability.

---

# Executive Governance Questions

The framework should repeatedly ask:

- Which business units face the greatest accessibility-driven customer risk?
- Where are inaccessible systems reducing strategic competitiveness?
- Which markets have increasing accessibility dependency?
- Which accessibility weaknesses threaten retention and trust?
- Are accessibility investments aligned with strategic growth opportunities?
- Which business units demonstrate the strongest inclusion maturity?
- Where are procurement requirements increasing accessibility pressure?
- Are innovation systems creating or reducing exclusion risk?
- Which areas require immediate accessibility transformation?
- Which accessibility capabilities could be scaled across the portfolio?

# Conclusion

This GE–McKinsey Nine-Box Matrix framework reframes accessibility and inclusion as strategic portfolio evaluation capabilities rather than isolated compliance activities.

Within this model:

- Industry Attractiveness evaluates how accessibility influences market opportunity, customer dependency, procurement competitiveness, and long-term demand.
- Business Strength evaluates the organisation’s operational, governance, innovation, and customer accessibility capability.

The framework is intended to support AI-assisted governance systems capable of:

- evaluating portfolio maturity
- prioritising investment
- identifying exclusion risk
- guiding transformation programmes
- supporting strategic planning
- improving governance maturity
- strengthening long-term competitiveness

Accessibility therefore becomes a measurable strategic portfolio capability that improves:

- customer trust
- operational resilience
- procurement competitiveness
- workforce sustainability
- innovation capacity
- long-term commercial stability
