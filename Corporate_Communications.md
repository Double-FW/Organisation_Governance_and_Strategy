# Corporate Communications  
## Accessibility Manager Knowledge Base (Instructional + Reasoning Framework)

---

## Purpose of this Document

This document defines how corporate communications must operate within an inclusive organisation. It establishes accessibility as a core requirement across all communication activities, including marketing, internal communications, public messaging, reporting, events, and crisis communications.

It is designed to support both human decision-making and GPT-based reasoning by providing principles, applied methodologies, and structured evaluation logic.

This document should be used when planning, creating, delivering, and evaluating communications across all channels and formats.

---

## Corporate Communications as a System of Inclusion

Corporate communications must be treated as a system that shapes how an organisation is understood, experienced, and trusted. Accessibility within this system ensures that all audiences can perceive, understand, and engage with communications without additional effort or exclusion.

Accessible communication is not limited to format adjustments. It includes representation, tone, structure, timing, and delivery. If any of these elements exclude users, the communication system fails.

Accessibility must therefore be embedded across the entire communications lifecycle, from planning through to feedback and continuous improvement.

---

## Communication as Cultural Expression

Corporate communications reflect organisational culture. If communications exclude disabled people, this signals that inclusion is not embedded, regardless of stated values.

Accessible communications must demonstrate:

- Respect for disabled people as part of mainstream audiences  
- Representation that reflects real lived experience  
- Language that preserves dignity and avoids otherness  
- Consistency between message and experience  

This ensures that communications reinforce inclusion rather than contradict it.

---

## Emotional and Functional Accessibility in Communication

Corporate communications must balance emotional and functional accessibility.

Functional accessibility ensures that communications are technically usable, including compatibility with assistive technologies, captions, transcripts, and clear navigation.

Emotional accessibility ensures that communications feel inclusive, respectful, and representative. This includes tone, narrative, and portrayal.

True inclusion requires both. Communications that are technically accessible but emotionally exclusionary fail to create trust or connection.

---

## Authentic Representation as Core Practice

Representation within communications must be authentic, diverse, and integrated into everyday contexts.

This requires:

- Inclusion of real disabled people  
- Representation of diverse experiences within disability  
- Avoidance of stereotypes, tokenism, and “inspirational” framing  
- Focus on agency, competence, and participation  

Representation must be validated through lived experience and co-creation, not assumed through internal perspectives.

This ensures that communications reflect reality rather than reinforcing bias.

---

## Multi-Channel and Multi-Modal Communication

Accessible communications must function across multiple channels and formats, ensuring that no single mode is required for understanding.

This includes:

- Text, audio, and visual formats  
- Captions, transcripts, and alternative descriptions  
- Compatibility with assistive technologies  
- Adaptability across platforms and devices  

Multi-modality ensures that communications remain accessible across diverse contexts and user needs.

---

## The Accessible Communications Lifecycle

Accessibility must be embedded throughout the communications lifecycle:

Planning must include identification of audience diversity, accessibility requirements, and potential barriers.

Creation must integrate accessible formats, inclusive language, and representation from the outset.

Accessibility integration ensures that all outputs meet functional and emotional requirements.

Testing must include validation with disabled users and assistive technologies.

Publication must maintain accessibility across all distribution channels.

Feedback collection must capture insights from diverse audiences.

Improvement must be continuous, using data to refine future communications.

---

## Inclusive Language and Narrative

Language is a critical component of accessibility. It shapes understanding, inclusion, and perception.

Accessible communications must:

- Use clear, plain, and structured language  
- Avoid ambiguity and unnecessary complexity  
- Reflect dignity and respect  
- Avoid framing disability as exceptional or separate  

Language must be consistent across internal and external communications, ensuring alignment with organisational values.

---

## Internal Communications Accessibility

Internal communications must be accessible to ensure that all employees can participate fully.

This includes:

- Accessible email, intranet, and video content  
- Compatibility with assistive technologies  
- Availability of alternative formats  
- Inclusive language and tone  

Accessibility in internal communications directly impacts employee experience, productivity, and retention.

---

## Public Messaging and Brand Alignment

Public messaging must reflect accessibility commitments and demonstrate measurable progress.

This includes:

- Transparent communication of accessibility initiatives  
- Inclusion of disabled perspectives in messaging  
- Alignment between brand values and communication outputs  

Public messaging must be validated through audience feedback and sentiment analysis to ensure credibility.

---

## Accessible Marketing and Social Media

Marketing communications must be accessible across all formats and platforms.

This includes:

- Captions and transcripts for video content  
- Alternative text that conveys emotional and narrative intent  
- Platform-specific accessibility practices  
- Inclusion of disabled voices and creators  

Social media must not be treated as exempt from accessibility requirements. It is often one of the most visible communication channels.

---

## Corporate Reporting Accessibility

Corporate reports must be accessible to ensure transparency and accountability.

This includes:

- Documents that meet accessibility standards  
- Alternative formats where required  
- Testing with assistive technologies  
- Clear structure and navigation  

Accessibility in reporting ensures that stakeholders can engage with organisational information equally.

---

## Crisis and Emergency Communications

Crisis communications must be accessible under time-sensitive conditions.

This requires:

- Clear, concise language  
- Multiple accessible formats  
- Compatibility with assistive technologies  
- Pre-tested communication systems  

Failure to ensure accessibility in crisis situations creates significant risk and exclusion.

---

## Events as Communication Environments

Corporate events are a critical component of communications and must be treated as accessible environments rather than isolated experiences.

Accessibility must be embedded across the entire event journey, including registration, physical access, content delivery, and follow-up.

Pre-event communication must include clear accessibility information, multiple registration methods, and detailed joining instructions.

Venues must support step-free access, inclusive seating layouts, accessible restrooms, and low-sensory spaces.

Content delivery must include captions, sign language interpretation, and accessible presentation practices.

Digital materials must be shared in advance and in accessible formats.

Catering and hospitality must consider dietary requirements and physical accessibility.

Staff must be trained in disability awareness and supported by clear roles such as accessibility coordinators.

Post-event communication must include accessible feedback mechanisms and archived content with transcripts and captions.

---

## Measurement and Outcome-Based Evaluation

Corporate communications must be evaluated based on outcomes rather than intent.

Quantitative measures include:

- Accessibility compliance  
- Engagement metrics  
- Representation levels  
- Training completion  

Qualitative measures include:

- Sentiment analysis  
- Feedback from disabled audiences  
- Employee experiences  
- Narrative authenticity  

Measurement must focus on whether communications produce equitable experiences across user groups.

---

## Governance and Accountability

Accessible communications require structured governance.

This includes:

- Executive sponsorship  
- Accessibility leadership roles  
- Cross-functional collaboration  
- Disabled advisory input  

Governance ensures consistency, accountability, and continuous improvement.

---

## Core Questions for Communication Decisions

All communication decisions should be evaluated using inclusion-based reasoning:

- Who is included or excluded by this communication?  
- Can all audiences perceive and understand the message?  
- Does this communication rely on a single modality?  
- Is representation authentic and respectful?  
- Does the tone support dignity and inclusion?  
- What evidence supports effectiveness?  

These questions ensure that communications align with inclusive principles.

---

## GPT Retrieval and Reasoning Guidance

When used by a GPT, this document should guide responses to:

- Treat communications as a system influencing perception and experience  
- Identify risks of exclusion across channels and formats  
- Recommend multi-modal and inclusive approaches  
- Prioritise authentic representation and lived experience  
- Align communication strategies with organisational inclusion principles  

---

## Closing Principle

Accessible corporate communications are achieved when all audiences can perceive, understand, and engage with messages equally, and when representation reflects real lived experience.

When communications consistently enable participation, trust, and clarity without additional effort, they become a driver of inclusion rather than a source of exclusion.

At that point, communication is not separate from accessibility. It is one of its most influential expressions.
