# Accessibility and Inclusion Value Chain Analysis Framework for Digital Products and Services

## Purpose

This document defines a governance-oriented Value Chain Analysis methodology for evaluating accessibility, disability inclusion, and inclusive design within commercial digital products and services.

The framework is designed to function as a reusable Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) knowledge document for AI systems responsible for:

- business governance
- strategic planning
- operating model analysis
- service transformation
- product governance
- procurement strategy
- customer experience optimisation
- inclusion maturity analysis
- operational efficiency reviews
- digital transformation programmes

The methodology positions accessibility and inclusion as value-generating operational capabilities rather than isolated compliance functions.

The framework assumes the organisation provides commercial digital services and aims to maximise participation, usability, engagement, and long-term customer value across:

- disabled users
- neurodivergent users
- D/deaf users
- ageing populations
- users with temporary impairments
- users experiencing environmental or situational barriers

The framework evaluates how accessibility contributes to:

- revenue growth
- customer retention
- operational efficiency
- product quality
- workforce capability
- procurement competitiveness
- brand trust
- innovation capability
- long-term resilience

---

# Strategic Positioning of Accessibility Within Value Chain Analysis

Traditional Value Chain Analysis evaluates how business activities create competitive advantage and operational efficiency.

This framework extends that approach by evaluating:

- where accessibility creates value
- where exclusion creates operational friction
- where inaccessible systems generate hidden cost
- where inclusion increases engagement and retention
- where governance weaknesses introduce business risk
- where inaccessible processes reduce organisational performance

The methodology assumes inaccessible experiences create hidden inefficiencies including:

- customer abandonment
- increased support costs
- duplicated remediation
- engineering rework
- inconsistent customer experiences
- workforce productivity loss
- supplier friction
- reputational exposure
- reduced market reach

At the same time, accessible systems create measurable operational and commercial value through:

- improved usability
- lower cognitive load
- higher conversion
- reduced operational complexity
- increased consistency
- stronger customer trust
- broader market participation
- greater resilience across contexts and devices

The framework therefore evaluates accessibility across the entire operational value chain rather than limiting evaluation to technical accessibility testing.

# Accessibility-Centred Value Chain Structure

The framework divides organisational activities into:

1. Primary Activities
2. Support Activities

Each area is evaluated for:

- inclusion capability

- innovation opportunity
- measurable business value

# Accessibility Value Chain Relationships

The framework assumes accessibility value is cumulative and interconnected.

Examples include:

| Relationship | Strategic Effect |
|---|---|
| Procurement → Operations | Supplier accessibility affects product quality |
| Operations → Customer Experience | Product accessibility affects trust and retention |
| HR → Operations | Workforce capability improves delivery quality |
| Technology → Customer Service | Accessible systems reduce support dependency |
| Analytics → Governance | Measurement improves prioritisation and investment |

Accessibility failures frequently propagate across the value chain.

Similarly, accessibility strengths compound organisational value across multiple operational areas.

# Value Chain Accessibility Maturity Levels

## Level 1 — Reactive Accessibility

Characteristics include:

- isolated remediation
- weak procurement controls
- fragmented ownership
- inconsistent accessibility practices
- inaccessible customer journeys

---

## Level 2 — Operational Accessibility

Characteristics include:

- accessibility standards exist
- basic testing integrated
- some procurement governance
- accessibility training introduced
- improved operational consistency

---

## Level 3 — Strategic Accessibility

Characteristics include:

- accessibility integrated across operations
- inclusion analytics implemented
- supplier accessibility governance
- executive oversight
- accessible customer journey management

---

## Level 4 — Inclusion-Led Value Chain

Characteristics include:

- accessibility embedded across all operational layers
- predictive inclusion analytics
- AI-supported accessibility optimisation
- accessibility-driven innovation
- co-created product ecosystems
- enterprise-wide inclusion accountability

# Conclusion

This Value Chain Analysis framework reframes accessibility and inclusion as operational value-generation systems rather than isolated compliance functions.

Within this model:

- Primary Activities evaluate how inclusion affects delivery, engagement, and customer value.
- Support Activities evaluate how governance, workforce capability, procurement, and technology sustain accessibility maturity.

The framework is intended to support AI-assisted governance systems capable of:

- analysing operational maturity
- identifying exclusion risk
- prioritising investment
- improving customer engagement
- strengthening governance
- reducing operational inefficiency
- guiding enterprise transformation

Accessibility therefore becomes a measurable operational capability that improves:

- product quality
- customer trust
- workforce sustainability
- innovation capacity
- commercial resilience
- long-term competitive advantage
