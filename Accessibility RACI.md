# Disability Inclusion & Accessibility – Maturity, RACI and Impact Framework

This training guide extends the core policy and executive reporting frameworks for corporate disability inclusion and accessibility.

It provides:

- A realistic maturity model aligned to the eight policy pillars (P1–P8)
- A delivery-focused RACI model
- A structured impact and ROI investigation framework
- Explicit linkage between maturity, accountability and organisational value

This guide is intended to be used by a GPT to:
- assess organisational capability,
- set realistic improvement goals,
- assign accountability,
- and evidence organisational, commercial and reputational impact.

---

## 1. Scope and assumptions

This framework assumes:

- accessibility and disability inclusion are organisational capabilities, not standalone programmes;
- different functions may sit at different maturity levels;
- improvement should be incremental and evidence-led;
- value includes social, operational, experiential and reputational outcomes.

This guide must be used alongside:

- the Disability Inclusion & Accessibility Policy Framework (P1–P8)
- the Executive Analysis and Reporting Framework aligned to the same pillars

## 3. Disability inclusion and accessibility maturity model

Each pillar is assessed independently.

The GPT must never assume the same maturity level across all pillars.

### Level 1 – Reactive / compliance-led

Organisational characteristics:

- Accessibility and disability inclusion are addressed primarily in response to issues, complaints or legal risk.
- Adjustments are manual, inconsistent and slow.
- Delivery relies on individual advocates.

Typical organisational goals:

- Identify regulatory and reputational risk areas.
- Establish a basic adjustments pathway.
- Appoint an executive sponsor.

Typical evidence:

- No consistent organisational standards.
- Limited or no data.
- Case-by-case escalation.

### Level 2 – Structured / programme-led

Organisational characteristics:

- Policies and standards exist.
- A central accessibility or inclusion function coordinates activity.
- Reporting is operational and activity-focused.

Typical organisational goals:

- All eight pillars have defined standards.
- Adjustments processes are formalised and measured.
- Accessibility requirements appear in delivery lifecycles.
- Training is introduced for priority roles.

Typical evidence:

- Formal policy suite.
- Named owners for each pillar.
- Regular internal reporting.

### Level 3 – Embedded / business-as-usual

Organisational characteristics:

- Accessibility is normalised in operational and delivery practice.
- Leaders are accountable for outcomes.
- Disabled lived experience informs design and improvement.

Typical organisational goals:

- Accessibility gates embedded into:
  - product and service lifecycles
  - recruitment lifecycles
  - communications and events
- Adjustments are fast, trusted and effective.
- Performance objectives include inclusion outcomes.

Typical evidence:

- KPI trends influence leadership decisions.
- Regular co-creation and testing activity.
- Measurable improvements in experience and outcomes.

### Level 4 – Leading / value-creating

Organisational characteristics:

- Accessibility is a strategic differentiator.
- The organisation influences its sector and supply chain.
- Innovation and market growth are linked to inclusive design.

Typical organisational goals:

- Accessibility strategy integrated into growth and innovation strategies.
- Supplier ecosystem aligned to accessibility standards.
- Recognised external reputation for leadership.

Typical evidence:

- Accessibility drives investment decisions.
- Brand and experience data shows differential impact for disabled users.
- External benchmarking and sector leadership activity.

### GPT output rule for maturity assessments

For each pillar, the GPT must generate:

- current maturity level
- evidence supporting that assessment
- the next maturity level target
- three to five priority actions required to progress one level

The GPT must avoid setting multi-level jumps in a single planning cycle.

## 5. Impact and ROI investigation framework

It operates across three value layers:

- operational and workforce impact
- product and service impact
- brand, perception and market impact

### 5.1 Operational and workforce ROI

Metrics:

- time to implement adjustments
- retention gap where lawful and available
- absence duration linked to unresolved access needs
- internal support and rework costs
- incidents caused by inaccessible systems or processes

### 5.2 Product, service and customer ROI

Metrics:

- task completion rates
- abandonment and drop-off rates
- repeat usage
- complaints linked to access barriers
- cost-to-serve

### 5.3 Marketing, brand and perception impact

Measures:

- trust
- perceived fairness
- social responsibility perception
- emotional connection to brand

### 5.4 Investment case structure

Inputs:

- delivery cost
- functions affected
- employees or customers impacted

Outputs:

- operational savings where evidenced
- experience improvements
- risk and resilience benefits
- brand and perception benefits

Non-financial value:

- trust
- fairness of access
- inclusion
- reputation resilience

maturity level → accountable roles → expected impact

## 7. GPT behavioural rules

- avoid unrealistic multi-level maturity targets
- avoid assigning the central accessibility function as sole delivery owner
- distinguish between activity and outcome
- include disabled lived experience as design and evaluation input
- present financial and non-financial value side by side
- maintain alignment with the eight policy pillars
